About us

A small pipeline, built to resist fooling itself

Signal & Noise started as an internal check on a simple question: when someone claims a model "predicts the market," what would it actually take to believe them? Most of the work turned out to be discipline, not modeling — keeping the train and test sets in time order, pricing in the cost of every trade, and comparing against the boring alternative of just holding the asset.

This site is that discipline made visible. The Stock ML Analysis page runs the same walk-forward pipeline as our underlying Python research script — live, in your browser — so you can change the market conditions and watch the model's edge appear, shrink, or vanish honestly.

No lookahead leakage

Every input to the model is knowable at the time the prediction is made — nothing computed from future prices sneaks into a feature.

Time-respecting evaluation

Data is split chronologically, never shuffled. The model trains on the past and is judged only on days it has never seen.

Costs before conclusions

Transaction costs are charged on every position change before we look at returns, so paper edges that can't survive trading don't get reported as wins.

Buy-and-hold is the opponent

A model is only interesting if it beats doing nothing. We show that comparison on every run, not just the ones that flatter the model.

What's under the hood
  • — pandas / numpy for feature engineering
  • — scikit-learn logistic regression & gradient boosting
  • — yfinance for historical price history
  • — walk-forward 70/30 train-test split
  • — cost-aware long/flat backtest vs. buy & hold
Who this is for

Anyone evaluating whether an ML trading claim holds up — analysts, students, and builders who want a transparent reference pipeline rather than a black box.

How this pipeline works

01

Generate data

A daily price series with tunable momentum/mean-reversion signal plus noise.

02

Engineer features

Returns, moving-average ratio, RSI, and realized volatility — all computed only from past prices.

03

Split by time

First 70% of days train the model; the last 30% test it. Never shuffled.

04

Train

Gradient descent fits a logistic regression to predict next-day direction.

05

Backtest

Predictions become long/flat positions, returns net of transaction cost, compared to buy & hold.
